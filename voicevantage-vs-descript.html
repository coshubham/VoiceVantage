Laptop (best 44) and cell phone (best 40) clones stayed below the pass line of 60. Low-quality landline (best 61) passes go to a Help Desk, not straight in.

AI voice cloning tools like Descript can copy a voice from seconds of audio, so a fair question follows: can a Descript clone slip past VoiceVantage? To answer it with evidence, the Institute for Cyber Security and Resilient Systems at Ontario Tech University (UOIT) tested VoiceCheck against the five most popular deepfake tools, including Descript. This page shows Descript's results in full.

How the test worked

1. Clone the voice

Descript was used to create deepfake copies of enrolled users' voices.

2. Try to log in

The clones tried to pass as the real user over a laptop microphone, a cell phone and an 8kHz landline.

3. VoiceCheck decides

Scores below 60 are rejected. A low pass under the 79 to 89 genuine range goes to a Help Desk.

Inside the 533-test study

The Ontario Tech study ran in two phases across all five deepfake tools. In every case, a score of 60 that stays below the 79 to 89 genuine range is referred to a Help Desk, not accepted.

What these results mean

Descript only reached the pass line on low landline audio, scoring 61 at best, below the genuine 79 to 89 range and Help-Desk-referred in production. On laptop and cell phone audio it did not pass.

Descript also has a built-in limit that works in your favor. It requires the person being cloned to read a specific consent script. An external attacker who has only a short recording of the target cannot produce that script in the target voice, so external attacks scored close to zero.

Where this protection matters

Contact centers

Callers are verified by voice in about 1.4 milliseconds, so agents are not left to judge a cloned voice by ear.

Banks and payments

A voice check before a payment or an account change adds a step that a cloned voice has to beat, and in testing it did not.

Help desks and resets

Password resets are a favourite target for voice fraud. Low scores go to a person for a second check instead of passing.

About Descript

Descript is a desktop application used widely for audio and video editing. Uniquely among the tools tested, it requires the person being cloned to read a consent script, which trains the model and limits misuse.

Why did Descript fail external attacks?
